Understanding the South Indian Village Interest System (Vaddi / వడ్డీ)

Across rural and suburban communities in Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Tamil Nadu (Vatti), and Karnataka, traditional credit financing operates under a unique localized accounting system: Interest per ₹100 per Month (నూటికి నెలకు వడ్డీ). Unlike commercial banking institutions that quote annualized percentage rates (APR), village moneylenders, farmers, and small business owners calculate interest on a monthly cash basis.

💡 The Fundamental Rule: Quoting "₹2 Vaddi" means paying ₹2 in interest for every ₹100 of borrowed principal each month. This represents 2% per month, translating directly to an annualized interest rate of $2\% \times 12 = 24\%\text{ per year}$.

Simple Vaddi vs. Chakra Vaddi (సాధారణ వడ్డీ vs. చక్రవడ్డీ)

Traditional village financial contracts divide into two core mathematical systems:

1. Simple Vaddi (సాధారణ వడ్డీ)

In a simple interest contract, the monthly interest rate applies solely to the initial principal borrowed (అసలు). Even if the loan spans multiple years without repayment, the interest does not compound:

Total Interest = Principal × Total Months × (Monthly Rate / 100)

2. Chakra Vaddi / Compound Interest (చక్రవడ్డీ)

Under a compound interest agreement (frequently known as Yedadi Tirugudu or annual rollover), accumulated unpaid interest is merged into the principal at fixed intervals (typically every 12 months or 6 months). For subsequent periods, interest accrues on this newly enlarged capital base:

  • Year 1: Interest is calculated on the original principal.
  • Rollover Date: Total Year 1 interest is added to the principal to form the new base.
  • Year 2: Interest is calculated on the compounded base amount.

How Date Durations and Partial Days Are Calculated

Calculating interest between two calendar dates requires counting full elapsed years, completed months, and remaining fractional days:

  1. The 30-Day Convention: In traditional Indian village accounting, 1 calendar month is standardized to exactly 30 days.
  2. Borrowing from Months: If the settlement day number is smaller than the start day, 30 days are borrowed from the months column.
  3. Fractional Calculation: If a loan runs for 1 year, 4 months, and 12 days, the duration converts into:
    Total Months = (1 × 12) + 4 + (12 / 30) = 16.40 Months.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How is ₹1.50 vaddi calculated on ₹50,000 for 8 months? +
At ₹1.50 vaddi (1.5% per month), the monthly interest is (₹50,000 × 1.5) / 100 = ₹750 per month. For 8 months, the total interest equals ₹750 × 8 = ₹6,000. Total repayment is ₹50,000 + ₹6,000 = ₹56,000.
Can I switch between monthly rate and annual percentage rate? +
Yes. Use the dropdown next to the rate input to switch between '₹ / ₹100 / Month' (standard village format) and '% Per Annum' (commercial bank format). The calculator automatically synchronizes the mathematical factors.
How does 1-year compounding (ఏడాది తిరుగుడు) work? +
Under annual compounding, exactly at month 12, the 12 months of accumulated simple interest is added to your original principal. In year 2, the interest rate is applied to this new, higher sum.
